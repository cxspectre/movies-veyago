## Do
1. **Scale-2 stills at the heaviest frames** of `Master4-wide` (3840x2160) and `Master4-tall` (2160x3840) with `FILM_GL=swangle`: the glass at 8:50 and 8:20-8:30 (the phone, Watch, five phones), the village visit at 7:20, the pull-back at 9:31-9:36, the ending pass and the dive at 9:42, the laptop on paper at 5:00, the crumple at 1:40, the held regions under a veil at 8:10. For each: seconds, peak memory (`/usr/bin/time -v`), and any failure: a canvas larger than the browser's limit, a WebGL texture over the GPU limit, an out-of-memory crash, a blank or half-drawn still. Compare the 4K look against the 1080p look of the same frame (the pencil physics, line width and paper texture must scale, not thin out).
2. **Review `tools/render.ts` for a 10-minute, 18,000-frame master at 4K**: the `--slots4` chunk ranges (write a test: they must tile frames 0-17999 exactly with no gap or overlap, and match `SLOTS4`), resume behaviour after a crash mid-chunk, disk use (estimate GB of chunks and of the final file at `--crf=14`), the concat step, the audio mux step (names and offsets), concurrency (4 cores on the cloud box versus a Mac: recommend `--concurrency` values for 8, 10 and 12 cores), and the one-command invocation. Fix real bugs you find; list the rest.
3. **`docs/v4/RENDER-PLAN.md`**: the exact commands in order for the owner's Mac to produce, for both formats: the 4K master video, the mixed master with audio, the 1080p proof (faster, for review first), the cut-downs (the G1 player is being built by another session: write the cut-down step as a stub that points to `docs/v4/CUTDOWNS.md` if it exists on `main`), the packaging; the expected time per step with your measured ratios and honest uncertainty, the disk and memory needs, what to check after each step, and how to resume. Say what in the render is most likely to fail or look wrong at 4K and the first thing to try.
